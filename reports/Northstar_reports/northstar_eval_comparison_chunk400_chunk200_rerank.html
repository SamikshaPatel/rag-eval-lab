Verdict — chunk200 (no reranking) remains the best overall configuration

Reranking with ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 did not improve results. The cross-encoder won zero metrics outright and introduced new retrieval failures that did not exist in chunk200 alone. NSB-003 (domestic wire limit) and NSB-026 (exchange rate monitoring) both went to prec=0.00, rec=0.00 after reranking — the reranker actively demoted the correct chunks.

Why it failed: ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 is trained on MS MARCO, a web-search dataset. It learns to score relevance for short factual web queries, not banking policy lookups. When the question is "What is the standard daily limit for domestic wires?" it may score a chunk about international wires (which mentions "standard daily limits") higher than the domestic-wire chunk, because the vocabulary overlap with "standard daily limit" is similar. Domain mismatch is the likely root cause.

What reranking did preserve: The multi-hop fixes from chunk200 (NSB-022, NSB-031) carried through — these questions benefit from focused 200-char chunks regardless of reranking. Multi-hop correctness remains at 1.00 in the rerank run.

Next options: (1) Try a domain-tuned reranker or fine-tune ms-marco on a few banking examples. (2) Increase k from 3→5 in chunk200 without reranking to recover the recall lost vs chunk400. (3) Address NSB-033 and NSB-023/024 via metadata-filtered retrieval targeting §1.1 and §3.2 directly.

Analysis

chunk200 is the current best

  • Wins on Ctx Precision (0.94), Ctx Relevancy (0.46), and Hallucination (0.53) — the three metrics most improved by tighter chunks.
  • Multi-hop correctness = 1.00 (same as rerank). NSB-022 and NSB-031 fixes are stable across both chunk200 configurations.
  • Faithfulness regression (0.79 → 0.67) is the main cost — 5 simple factual questions lose grounding when answers span chunk boundaries.
  • Recommended next step: Try chunk200 with k=5 to recover the recall and faithfulness losses without sacrificing the relevancy gains.

Why reranking hurt

  • Domain mismatch: ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 is trained on web-search data. Banking policy language ("standard daily limit", "Dual Approval threshold") does not match the query-document patterns the model learned.
  • New retrieval failures: NSB-003 and NSB-026 went from working to complete failure (prec=0.00, rec=0.00). The reranker pushed the correct chunks out of the top 3.
  • False precision: The reranker sometimes scores high on surface-level keyword overlap rather than semantic relevance — e.g. a chunk mentioning both "wire" and "daily limit" outscores the chunk that actually states the domestic wire limit.
  • Reranking can work well, but requires either a domain-tuned model or a larger candidate pool (k=20+) so the correct chunk is unlikely to be dropped.

Persistent failures (all 3 runs)

  • NSB-033 (Viewer vs User): prec=0.00, rec=0.00 in all three runs. The role-definition text for Viewer and User share near-identical embedding vectors — the retriever consistently fails to surface §1.1.
  • NSB-023/024 (Exception policy): Retrieval is now correct in the rerank run (NSB-023 faith=1.00 in rerank!), but correctness remains 0.00 — the model retrieves the right chunk but still gives the wrong answer. This is a generation/prompt problem, not retrieval.
  • These require targeted fixes: metadata routing for NSB-033, and a negative-constraint prompt instruction for NSB-023/024 ("if the rule says 'always', no amount-based exception applies").

Recommended next experiments

  • chunk200, k=5 — most likely to recover faithfulness losses while keeping relevancy gains. Low effort, no new dependencies.
  • Semantic / sentence-boundary chunking — eliminates the chunk-boundary faithfulness issue by splitting on natural sentence breaks rather than fixed char counts.
  • Domain-tuned reranker — fine-tune ms-marco on (question, passage) pairs from the Northstar golden set. Even 36 examples may help. Higher effort but addresses the root cause of reranking failure.
  • Prompt fix for exception questions — add an explicit instruction: "If the handbook states a rule applies 'regardless of amount' or 'always', treat it as an absolute constraint with no exceptions."
